# #1781 Keep a thread card's margin row clear only where that costs no width

A thread card beside its words stood past the margin row for its target whenever the room beyond that row still held the card's 320px minimum (`--thread-card-min`), so a row of one or two controls narrowed the card by their width. On an Ask at a 1600px window the card was 341px wide with 424px free beside the words, and at 1440 it covered the row and came out wider (344px) than at 1600.

`comment-placement.js` now keeps the row clear only where the room past it holds the card's whole measure (`--thread-card`, 592px). Anywhere narrower, the card stands over the row, beside the words: it carries its own close and step controls, and the row shows again once it closes. The comment box uses the same rule, so Send still leaves the card where the box stood. The row is one box to the rule, so nothing declares which margin items may be covered; this settles the TODO entry that asked for such a mechanism.

`commentPlacement` also reads both card widths itself. Its callers used to pass `minimumWidth: cardMinimum()` while the placement read `cardMeasure()` on its own, and #1526 began as the box and the card being placed from two different minimum widths.

| Window | Card before | Card after |
|---|---|---|
| 1440 | 344 (over the row) | 344 |
| 1600 | 341 | 424 |
| 1728 | 405 | 488 |
| 2200 | 592 (past the row) | 592 (past the row) |

Behaviour that moved, with the tests that record it:

- At 1600 in the feature gallery the card no longer keeps a gap past the pin on its words: it starts 8px from the words' edge, over whatever of the pin reaches that far, and takes the room from there (`test_a_thread_beside_its_words_takes_the_room_to_the_visible_edge`).
- At 1920 the margin-answered thread covers its cluster; a 2400 case keeps the past-the-row branch covered (`test_a_thread_can_be_answered_in_the_margin_without_opening_threads`).
- A Node test pins the rule at its owner: a row is kept clear with 592px past it and covered with 591px (`comment-placement.test.mjs`).

All three fail on the old minimum-width rule.

The rule reads the card's declared measure rather than its content, so a short thread that would fit past the row still covers it. Choosing by content width would let the card move on Send and jump sides as replies arrive.
